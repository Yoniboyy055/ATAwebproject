# UNESCO Publication-Precondition Register — Claude WP-04 v0.1

**Status:** These preconditions remain **in force**. The WP-03 merge did not lift any of them.
**Restated because it is the single most likely control to be lost by accident.**

---

## 1. The precondition, stated once and plainly

> **No UNESCO claim may be published until a human has opened `https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1550/` and confirmed the exact official wording.**

Everything else in this register follows from that.

## 2. Why the precondition exists

`whc.unesco.org` returned **HTTP 403** to every direct request from the research environment — repeatedly, across three different paths, using two different tools. The agent proxy was healthy, so the block is at the destination.

Research therefore proceeded via **search-engine extracts of UNESCO's own pages**, corroborated against the EU EEAS, University College London, and Eritrean Ministry of Information. Those sources agree with each other, and the facts are assessed high confidence.

**But high confidence from corroboration is not the same as having read the source.** No UNESCO claim in this project is marked primary-source verified, because that standard was not achieved. Recording that honestly is the whole point.

## 3. What the check costs, and what it unlocks

**Cost:** one person, one browser, about five minutes.

**Unlocks:** the Asmara destination description, the tour overview's UNESCO statement, and dashboard rows D-01 and D-03 — the strongest heritage content the site has.

This is the highest-value, lowest-effort action available anywhere in the project.

## 5. Prohibited UNESCO language — permanent, not conditional

These do not become permissible after the source check. They are wrong regardless.

| Never use | Why |
|---|---|
| "UNESCO-like architecture" | Inaccurate and self-undermining — understates real status while implying something unverified |
| "UNESCO-approved" / "UNESCO-endorsed" / "UNESCO partner" | UNESCO endorses no commercial operator |
| Any UNESCO logo or emblem | Emblem use is restricted; requires permission and professional review |
| Calling any individual building "UNESCO-listed" | Fiat Tagliero, Cinema Impero and the Cathedral are **contributing elements**, not separate inscriptions |
| "All of Asmara is a World Heritage Site" | The property is a bounded historic perimeter, not the municipality |
| "UNESCO World Heritage Site of Eritrea" | The property is Asmara's historic centre, not the country |
| "UNESCO-certified guides" | No such certification exists |
| World Heritage status used to imply safety, quality or service standards | Inscription is a heritage-protection status only |

## 6. The correction that matters most

The inscription is **urban**. What UNESCO recognised is the *planned modernist city as a whole* — its layout, zoning and coherent building stock inside a defined perimeter. It is **not** a list of individually certified monuments.

This matters commercially as well as factually: the honest version is a **stronger** story than the mockup's vague "UNESCO-like architecture". Asmara genuinely holds World Heritage inscription. Saying so accurately is better copy than hedging.

## 7. Cultural-sensitivity precondition

U-13 records that UNESCO's own description notes planning based on functional **and racial** zoning. The inscribed heritage is therefore explicitly colonial heritage, including its segregationist planning.

For a diaspora-facing product this requires care:

- Do not romanticise the colonial period or credit Italy alone. UNESCO's criterion (ii) itself emphasises **local morphologies, methods, materials, skills and labour** — Eritrean hands built it.
- Avoid "Little Rome" and "a slice of Italy in Africa". They erase Eritrean agency and can read as celebratory of colonisation to exactly the audience ATA is courting.
- Preferred framing: an Eritrean city that absorbed, adapted and now stewards a modernist inheritance, with Eritrean labour named and the indigenous quarter acknowledged as part of the inscribed property.

**ATA approval plus an Eritrean cultural reviewer is recommended** before any historical narration is published.
