# Asmara–Massawa Escarpment and Journey Fact Sheet — Claude WP-03 v0.1

**Status:** Internal YK Systems research — **not ATA-approved; no wording herein is publication-released**
**Purpose:** supply the factual basis for the flagship's signature segment (WP-02 **M-18** overview, **M-20** day-3 itinerary, and the escarpment element of the difficulty statement **M-15**).

---

## 1. Why this segment matters

WP-01 identified the highlands-to-Red-Sea contrast as the strongest structural story in the whole reference pack — and, unusually for this project, one resting on **geography rather than unverified operational claims**. The research supports that: the descent is genuinely remarkable, and it can be described vividly without ATA confirming anything commercial.

## 3. The journey-time trap

**ES-06 must not become customer copy.** A generic distance calculator's "1 h 39 min" is a straight-line-speed estimate. A real descent of 2,300 m on a switchback road — with photo stops, village stops and lunch, as the reference itinerary itself implies — is a materially longer day.

Publishing an under-estimate would be a customer-experience failure and, arguably, a misleading claim about ATA's product.

**Rule:** journey duration is an **ATA operational fact** (WP-01 V-009). Research may state the *distance* with hedging and the *elevation change* with confidence; **ATA states the time.**

## 4. Proposed wording (DRAFT — Claude synthesis, ATA approval required)

### 4.1 Overview / long-form (resolves the `{altitude_change}` slot in WP-02 M-18)
> "Between Asmara and Massawa the road falls about 2,300 metres. You start the morning on a highland plateau at roughly 2,325 metres, in air that is cool enough for a jacket, and you finish it at sea level on the Red Sea. The road descends the escarpment in switchbacks, and the landscape changes as you go — highland terraces giving way to dry slopes, then to the coastal plain and the salt light of the sea."

### 4.2 Short / facts-row use
> "A 2,300 m descent from highland plateau to Red Sea coast."

### 4.3 Itinerary day-3 descriptive layer
> "**Day 3 — Down the escarpment.** The drive from Asmara to the coast, descending roughly 2,300 metres through switchbacks and changing country."
Activity lines beyond this (specific stops, villages, timings) remain **[ATA CONFIRMATION REQUIRED]**.

### 4.4 Historical colour (optional)
> "Until the twentieth century's engineering arrived, moving goods up this escarpment was the hard problem of Eritrean trade — the Italians eventually strung a 72 km cableway between Massawa and Asmara in 1937."
**Conditional** — accurate as history, but must not imply the cableway is a current attraction.

### 4.5 Difficulty / suitability contribution (M-15)
Research supports describing the segment as **a long seated mountain drive with sustained descent and continuous curves**. That is honest and useful: it signals motion-sickness relevance and vehicle-comfort relevance without making a fitness claim.

**Draft:** "The escarpment drive is a long stretch in the vehicle, with continuous curves and a sustained descent."

**Everything else about the drive is ATA's:** vehicle type and air-conditioning, driver, actual duration, rest stops, road condition on the day, and suitability for elderly travellers or young children (WP-01 T-05, T-10). Research **cannot** answer "are the roads safe and comfortable?" (WP-02 FAQ **F-05**) — only the geometry half of it.

## 5. Prohibited / high-risk wording

| Do not use | Why |
|---|---|
| Any specific journey duration | Operational; research source unreliable (ES-06) |
| "Hundreds of hairpin bends" | Not a verified count |
| "Smooth, well-paved road" / any road-condition assurance | Unverified; changes over time; ATA-only |
| "Safe drive" / safety assurances | Safety claim — ATA + professional review |
| "Ride the historic cableway" or similar | The cableway does not operate |
| "Africa's most spectacular drive" and similar superlatives | Unverifiable comparative claim |
| "Easy" as a blanket descriptor for the day | Contradicts a long mountain descent; V-018 discipline |
