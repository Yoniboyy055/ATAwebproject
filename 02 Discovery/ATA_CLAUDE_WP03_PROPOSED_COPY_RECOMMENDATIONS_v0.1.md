## 1. UNESCO — replacing the banned phrase

**Concise (destination card / hero-adjacent):**
> Asmara's historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — inscribed in 2017 as *Asmara: A Modernist African City*.

**Tight mobile variant:**
> A UNESCO World Heritage city centre, inscribed in 2017.

**Precise (Important Information):**
> Asmara's historic centre is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as *Asmara: A Modernist African City* (2017, criteria ii and iv). The inscription recognises the planned modernist city as a whole — its early-20th-century layout and its coherent stock of rationalist buildings — rather than listing individual buildings separately.

**Extended (overview):** see UNESCO brief §4.3.

⚠️ All four carry the same precondition: confirm exact official phrasing at `whc.unesco.org/en/list/1550/`.

## 2. Asmara

**Short:** "Eritrea's highland capital at about 2,325 m — a planned modernist city whose historic centre UNESCO inscribed on the World Heritage List in 2017."

**Medium:** "Asmara sits high on Eritrea's central plateau, and it looks like nowhere else. Laid out in phases between 1893 and 1941, its historic centre is a near-complete early modernist city — cinemas, workshops, churches and cafés in rationalist concrete — inscribed by UNESCO on the World Heritage List in 2017. It is a city best seen at walking pace."

- "The Lombard-Romanesque Catholic cathedral on Harnet Avenue." *(date omitted — source disagreement; name pending ATA choice)*

- "Harnet Avenue — Asmara's palm-lined main street, and the spine of the historic centre."

## 3. Massawa

**Short:** "Eritrea's historic Red Sea port — an old town built from coral stone, layered with Ottoman, Egyptian and Italian history."

**Medium:** "Massawa is Eritrea's Red Sea port, and its old town sits on a coral island. Ottoman, Egyptian and Italian builders each left their mark here, much of it cut from coral quarried out of the reef. The independence war left its mark too — parts of the old town still stand as ruins. Hot, salt-aired and quiet, it is the opposite of Asmara in almost every way."

**Honest condition disclosure:** see Massawa fact sheet §2. **This is the single most important copy recommendation in the package** — it converts a concealed disappointment into a genuine reason to go.

## 4. The escarpment

**Overview:** "Between Asmara and Massawa the road falls about 2,300 metres. You start the morning on a highland plateau at roughly 2,325 metres, in air cool enough for a jacket, and you finish it at sea level on the Red Sea. The road descends the escarpment in switchbacks, and the landscape changes as you go — highland terraces giving way to dry slopes, then to the coastal plain and the salt light of the sea."

**Short:** "A 2,300 m descent from highland plateau to Red Sea coast."

**Day-3 frame:** "**Day 3 — Down the escarpment.** The drive from Asmara to the coast, descending roughly 2,300 metres through switchbacks and changing country."

**Optional history:** "Until modern engineering arrived, moving goods up this escarpment was the hard problem of Eritrean trade — the Italians eventually strung a 72 km cableway between Massawa and Asmara in 1937." *(must not imply current operation)*

## 5. Climate — the two-climate frame

> **Two climates, one journey.**
> Asmara sits at about 2,325 m: mild and temperate all year, cool in the evenings, with most of its rain falling between June and September.
> Massawa is at sea level on the Red Sea: warm through the year and genuinely hot in mid-summer, when daytime temperatures commonly reach the high thirties to low forties Celsius.
> Pack for both.

**Packing intro:** "Pack for two climates: cool highland evenings in Asmara, and hot coastal days in Massawa."
