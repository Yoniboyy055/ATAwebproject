# Source-Quality Standard — Claude WP-03 v0.1

**Status:** Internal YK Systems record. Defines what counts as an acceptable source for ATA public content.

---

## 1. Source hierarchy

| Tier | Source class | Use for |
|---:|---|---|
| **1** | UNESCO and authoritative international institutions (UNESCO, FAO, World Bank, WMO, UN bodies) | Heritage designations, climate regimes, development/geographic baselines. **Required** for any UNESCO or climate-regime claim |
| **2** | Eritrean government / official records (Ministry of Information, Ministry of Tourism, embassies) | National facts, official designations, entry requirements. Official but **not neutral** — acceptable for factual records, treat promotional framing as promotional |
| **3** | Recognised academic, historical, geographic, climate and architectural sources (peer-reviewed journals, university outputs, Archnet, museum/institutional records, encyclopaedias of record) | Architectural attribution, dates, historical narrative |
| **4** | Highly reputable secondary sources (established reference works, quality press, climate-data aggregators with transparent method) | Corroboration; station-level climate averages; filling gaps where tiers 1–3 are silent |
| **✗** | Generic travel blogs, SEO travel-content sites, AI-generated aggregators, user-review platforms, marketing copy of other operators | **Not acceptable for load-bearing claims.** Usable only as a pointer toward a better source, never cited as the basis of a published fact |

## 2. Rules applied in this package

1. **Highest available tier wins.** A tier-4 figure is not published if a tier-1 source addresses the same fact.
2. **Two independent sources** for any material claim, or the claim is confidence-capped and marked single-source.
3. **Load-bearing claims require tier 1–3.** A "load-bearing" claim is one a customer could rely on when deciding to travel or spend: heritage status, climate, safety-adjacent facts, entry requirements.
4. **Promotional adjectives are never facts.** "Pristine," "untouched," "world-class," "hidden gem" were rejected wherever encountered, including from tier-2 official media.
5. **Superlatives require a source that measures them.** Absent that, they are cut.
6. **Comparative claims** ("best-preserved in Africa," "one of the finest") are attributed to whoever said them or dropped — never asserted in ATA's voice.
7. **Time-sensitive claims** get an explicit review interval. Building condition, opening status, entry requirements and road status decay fastest.
8. **Numbers are hedged or omitted.** Climate averages are published as approximations, never decimals. Population figures with wide inter-source variance are omitted entirely.
9. **Operational facts are never sourced externally.** If a claim is about what ATA does or provides, no external source can validate it. It routes to ATA.

## 3. Disqualifying patterns encountered

During this research the following were found and excluded from load-bearing use:

- **AI-generated travel aggregators** presenting confident, uncited destination "facts" — several appeared in results for Massawa architecture and Eritrean climate.
- **Content-farm "best time to visit" pages** offering single-season recommendations for Eritrea that ignore the three-rainy-season structure. These would have reproduced exactly the V-014 error.
- **Distance/route calculators** presenting algorithmic driving times for mountain roads as though authoritative (see escarpment brief ES-06).
- **One internally inconsistent climate figure** for Massawa precipitation, rejected outright (see climate brief §4).
- **Review platforms** offering opening hours and access for the National Museum — excluded; operational status is ATA's to confirm.

## 4. Standing recommendation

Any future ATA content research — by any agent or contributor — should apply this hierarchy, and should record for every published fact: the source, its tier, the confidence level, and the review date. That record is what allows ATA to defend its own website copy later.
