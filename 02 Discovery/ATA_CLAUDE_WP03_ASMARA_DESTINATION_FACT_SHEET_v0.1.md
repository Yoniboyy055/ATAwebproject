## 2. Named sites in the reference itinerary

These are the sites the reference itinerary (SRC-UI-003 Day 2) lists. Research below establishes **what each place is**; it does **not** establish that ATA can access, enter, or include any of them — that is an ATA operational confirmation (WP-01 V-009).

### Fiat Tagliero
| Attribute | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| What | Former service station / petrol station | High |
| Built | **1938** (construction 1937–38) | High |
| Designer | **Giuseppe Pettazzi**, Italian engineer | High |
| Style | Futurist; widely cited as a landmark of Asmara's rationalist architecture | High |
| Form | Reinforced concrete, modelled on an aeroplane with cantilevered "wings" | High |
| Context | Associated with Futurist "aerial architecture" ideas circulating in the 1930s | Medium |

**Draft wording:** "The Fiat Tagliero service station (1938) — Giuseppe Pettazzi's concrete aeroplane, wings outstretched over a forecourt."

### Cinema Impero
| Attribute | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| What | Art Deco cinema, on Harnet Avenue | High |
| Built | **1937** | High |
| Designer | **Mario Messina** | High |
| Note | The largest cinema built in Asmara in the late colonial period; frequently cited among the world's finest surviving Art Deco cinemas | Medium-High (the "world's finest" framing is a widely-repeated characterisation — attribute it, don't assert it) |

**Draft wording:** "Cinema Impero (1937), Mario Messina's Art Deco cinema on Harnet Avenue — widely described by architectural writers as one of the most complete surviving cinemas of its kind."
**Note:** the comparative is **attributed, not asserted** (P-17). Dropping the clause entirely is also acceptable and slightly safer.

### Asmara Cathedral (Church of Our Lady of the Rosary)
| Attribute | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Style | **Lombard-Romanesque**; often described as among the finest such churches outside Italy | Medium-High |
| Architect | **Mario Mazzetti** | Medium |
| Date | ⚠️ **SOURCE DISAGREEMENT** — one source: built 1922. Another: constructed 1921–1923, inaugurated 1923 | **Low-Medium** |

**Action:** do not publish a construction date until resolved. Safe wording omits the date: "the Lombard-Romanesque Catholic cathedral on Harnet Avenue." Also note the building is referred to variously as Asmara Cathedral, St Joseph's Cathedral, and the Church of Our Lady of the Rosary — **ATA should confirm the name it wishes to use**, ideally the one locals use.

### Medeber Market
| Attribute | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| What | Working market and artisan quarter where scrap metal, rubber, wood and plastic are recycled into new goods — utensils, furniture, bicycles, appliances | High |
| Also | Associated with spice trade including berbere | Medium |

**Draft wording:** "Medeber, where Asmara's craftspeople turn salvaged metal into new tools, pots and furniture — a working workshop quarter, not a souvenir market."
**Sensitivity note:** this is a working industrial site and people's livelihood. Copy should avoid poverty-tourism framing; photography etiquette is an ATA operational/guide matter.

### National Museum of Eritrea
| Attribute | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Established | **1992** | Medium |
| History | Originally housed in the former Governor's Palace until **1997**, then relocated | Medium |
| Holdings | Historical artefacts, cultural collections from local sites; conducts archaeological survey and excavation | Medium |
| **Current opening status** | **NOT VERIFIED** | — |

⚠️ **Do not state or imply the museum is open, or that a visit is included.** Opening status, hours and access are exactly the kind of operational fact WP-01 V-009 requires ATA to confirm. Museum access in Eritrea has been reported as variable.

### Harnet Avenue
| Attribute | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| What | Asmara's main avenue (also rendered Liberation Avenue), lined chiefly with buildings of the Italian period | High |
| Role | A principal element of the inscribed urban landscape | Medium-High |

**Draft wording:** "Harnet Avenue — Asmara's palm-lined main street, and the spine of the historic centre."

## 3. Proposed destination blurb (DRAFT — resolves WP-02 M-07 Asmara slot)

**Short (destination card, ≤2 lines):**
> "Eritrea's highland capital at about 2,325 m — a planned modernist city whose historic centre UNESCO inscribed on the World Heritage List in 2017."

**Medium (homepage destination block):**
> "Asmara sits high on Eritrea's central plateau, and it looks like nowhere else. Laid out in phases between 1893 and 1941, its historic centre is a near-complete early modernist city — cinemas, workshops, churches and cafés in rationalist concrete — inscribed by UNESCO on the World Heritage List in 2017. It is a city best seen at walking pace."

Both are **conditional** on the UNESCO primary-source confirmation in the UNESCO brief.

## 4. Rejected / withheld claims

| Claim | Why withheld |
|---|---|
| A specific population figure | Sources vary from ~783 k to ~1.15 m depending on year and city-vs-metro definition. Publishing any single number risks being wrong; the copy does not need it |
| "Little Rome" / "Africa's Little Rome" | Widely used in travel writing but colonial-nostalgic framing; erases Eritrean agency. Recommended against for a diaspora audience (see UNESCO brief §5) |
| Cathedral construction date | Source disagreement (see §2) |
| "Africa's best-preserved colonial city" and similar superlatives | Unverifiable comparative claim |
| Museum open / visitable | Operational fact — ATA only |
| Any claim that named buildings are individually UNESCO-listed | False (see UNESCO brief §3) |
| Café/coffee-culture claims as ATA inclusions | Describing Asmara's café culture generally is fine; asserting ATA provides it is operational |
